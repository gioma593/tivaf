TIVAF – Technical framework

Tourism‑Institutional Value Appropriation Framework
A mixed‑methods diagnostic for value capture in data‑poor, small‑firm wine regions and GI territories.

1. Problem statement

Commercial databases (e.g., Orbis) systematically under‑represent small wineries. Official statistics rarely distinguish tourism from non‑tourism sales. Conventional hedonic pricing models require large cross‑sections unavailable in many appellations. TIVAF addresses this gap by providing an operational, replicable diagnostic that works with small, stratified samples (N ≈ 20).

2. Theoretical grounding

TIVAF integrates three literatures:

  • New Institutional Economics – institutions as rulebooks (consortia, appellation rules, transaction costs).
  • Place‑based development – institutional thickness, social capital, collective learning.
  • Hedonic price theory – extended to include tourism status, digital narratives, and institutional indices.

The framework embeds econometric exploration within an analytic narrative of regional institutional evolution (Bates et al., 1998).

3. Four pillars (operationalization)

Pillar 1 – Institutional‑thickness audit

Likert‑type rubric (1–5) across three domains: (i) network integration (shared infrastructure, storytelling, inter‑organizational signaling); (ii) symbolic capital (territorial branding, experiential realm, heritage materiality); (iii) transaction‑cost indicators (information accessibility, service efficiency, DTC logistics).

Output: Winery‑level Institutional Thickness Index (median of 9 items). Inter‑rater reliability: weighted Cohen's κ > 0.60.

Pillar 2 – Multi‑channel price mapping

Records price of a representative entry‑level wine (same label, 0.75L, base tier) across three channels: GDO/supermarket, specialist wine shop, winery door (DTC). Absence coded as “strategic retail bypass”.

Derived indicators: Information Premium = (Pspecialist – PGDO)/PGDO; Tourism Premium = (PDTC – PGDO)/PGDO.

Pillar 3 – Digital terroir & narrative scoring

Content analysis of website + social media (12‑month window). Four indicators (1–5): narrative focus (territory vs. product), information density (technical sheets, maps, booking), engagement depth (comments+saves/likes), experiential realm (sensory storytelling vs. aesthetic imagery).

Output: Composite Digital Identity Score (average of four, rescaled 1–5).

Pillar 4 – Hedonic pricing under data opacity

Log‑linear OLS model with heteroskedasticity‑robust standard errors:

ln(Pic) = α + β1 Tourismi + β2 Digitali + β3 Thicknessi + γ' Attributesi + δ Regioni + εic

Where Tourism is a binary (structured tourism offer), Digital and Thickness are indices from Pillars 3 & 1, Attributes includes organic certification and appellation sub‑category, and Region captures Pignoletto vs. Priorat.

Robustness: Variance inflation factors, leave‑one‑out sensitivity, separate models by channel.

Interpretation: Exploratory associations, not causal. Coefficients approximate percentage differences in price.

4. Case selection & sampling

Contrastive case design: Pignoletto DOCG (Colli Bolognesi, Italy) and Priorat DOQ (Catalonia, Spain). Stratified sample of 20 wineries (10 per region; 5 tourism‑integrated, 5 non‑tourism). Sampling based on producer mappings and fieldwork residencies. Small‑N design appropriate given under‑representation of small wineries in commercial data.

5. Replicability & output

TIVAF includes a codebook, observation forms, price mapping sheets, digital scoring rubrics, and inter‑rater reliability protocol. It generates:

  • Winery‑level indices (institutional thickness, digital identity).
  • Channel price gaps (information premium, tourism premium).
  • Exploratory hedonic estimates for governance diagnostics.

6. Limitations

  • Not a causal treatment‑effect estimator.
  • Small N limits generalizability.
  • Omits vintage and critic scores (mitigated by focusing on entry‑level wines).
  • Requires field access and two coders for reliability.
